Nvidia RTX 60 GPUs may slip to 2028

A new leak claims Nvidia’s RTX 60 consumer GPUs may not arrive until 2028, extending the wait for its next gaming graphics generation.

Abbas Jaffar Ali
3 min read
Sep 21, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Hardware leaker Kopite7Kimi claims Nvidia’s RTX 60 generation has been postponed to 2028.
  • The report is unconfirmed, and Nvidia has not announced a consumer launch date.
  • AI accelerator demand is being linked to the possible delay, but Nvidia’s official Rubin material covers data-centre hardware.

Nvidia RTX 60 graphics cards may not arrive until 2028 after another reported delay, according to hardware leaker Kopite7Kimi. The claim is unconfirmed, but it would leave the current RTX 50 generation carrying Nvidia’s consumer GPU line for longer than expected.

The leaker posted that GR20x — believed to refer to a future Rubin-based consumer GPU — is now a 2028 product and has been postponed again. Tom’s Hardware reports the claim, while TechSpot says other reports also point to 2028. Nvidia has not announced a GeForce RTX 60 launch date.

Nvidia RTX 60 could follow a longer RTX 50 cycle

Nvidia’s RTX 50 series launched with the Blackwell architecture in January 2025. A 2028 RTX 60 launch would put roughly three years between the two consumer generations, assuming the rumoured date applies to the first cards rather than the complete product family.

Earlier leaks had pointed to a 2027 window, with some reports narrowing that to the second half of the year. The latest claim pushes the timing back again, although it does not establish whether Nvidia plans to launch a small number of cards first or delay the whole range.

The current GeForce RTX 5090 remains the top-end consumer reference point while the next generation is unconfirmed. Nvidia’s official Rubin material is focused on data-centre systems, including Rubin GPUs and rack-scale AI platforms; it does not confirm a consumer GeForce product or an RTX 60 schedule.

Why data-centre demand is part of the RTX 60 rumour

The reported delay is being linked to Nvidia’s focus on AI accelerators. Data-centre products command much higher prices than gaming graphics cards, and Rubin is already being positioned by Nvidia as a platform for large AI systems. That does not prove consumer GPU production is being cut, but it explains why supply, engineering capacity and advanced manufacturing could be prioritised elsewhere.

For people building PCs in the UAE, the practical effect is limited to timing for now. There is no confirmed RTX 60 price, release date or UAE availability to plan around, and Nvidia’s current cards remain the only firm reference point. Anyone buying today should compare current RTX 50 pricing and performance, including [how the RTX 5050 compares with older GeForce cards](https://tbreak.com/gtx-1080-vs-rtx-5050-decade-gpu-comparison/), rather than waiting for a launch window that may move again.

Nvidia has not commented on the report. Until the company announces a product or provides a roadmap, the 2028 timing remains a leak rather than a release plan.

Is Nvidia RTX 60 confirmed for 2028?

No. The 2028 timing comes from an unconfirmed hardware leak, and Nvidia has not announced an RTX 60 release date.

What is the current Nvidia consumer GPU generation?

Nvidia’s current consumer generation is the GeForce RTX 50 series, based on the Blackwell architecture.

Why could AI affect the RTX 60 launch?

AI data-centre products are a major part of Nvidia’s business, so supply and engineering priorities could favour accelerators over consumer graphics cards. This remains an explanation for the rumour, not a confirmed reason.
